# Research: how the hot air balloon was invented (angle: smoke, a box that hit the ceiling, then a sheep, a duck and a rooster flew first)

## Key facts
- In 1782 Joseph Montgolfier sat by a fireplace and watched sparks and smoke rise up the flue. He wondered whether heated air could lift a vehicle.
  Source: https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/artificial-clouds-and-inflammable-air-the-science-and-spectacle-of-the-first-balloon-flights-1783/
- First experiment: he built a light wooden frame covered with taffeta (a silk cloth), burned paper at its lower opening, and it rose to the ceiling. Wikipedia dates this to November 1782 in Avignon.
  Sources: https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/artificial-clouds-and-inflammable-air-the-science-and-spectacle-of-the-first-balloon-flights-1783/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgolfier_brothers
- The brothers (Joseph-Michel and Jacques-Étienne) came from a paper-making family. They believed the smoke did the lifting; the real cause was hot air.
  Source: https://www.chm.bris.ac.uk/webprojects2003/hetherington/final/montgolfier_bros.html (via search summary) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgolfier_brothers
- First public demonstration: 4 June 1783, Annonay (their home town), an unmanned balloon about 10.5 m across, heated by a straw fire.
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgolfier_brothers ; https://www.space.com/16595-montgolfiers-first-balloon-flight.html
- 19 September 1783, Versailles, "before King Louis XVI and Queen Marie Antoinette and a crowd": the balloon Aérostat Réveillon carried a sheep (named Montauciel, "climb to the sky"), a duck and a rooster.
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgolfier_brothers ; https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/first-living-creatures-to-take-flight-in-a-man-made-craft
- Why animals: "There was some concern about the effects of flight into the upper atmosphere on living creatures." The sheep stood in for a human.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgolfier_brothers
- The flight lasted about eight minutes, covered about 3 km, and landed in a nearby wood; all three animals were "alive and well".
  Sources: https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/artificial-clouds-and-inflammable-air-the-science-and-spectacle-of-the-first-balloon-flights-1783/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgolfier_brothers
- First human free flight: 21 November 1783, Pilâtre de Rozier and the marquis d'Arlandes, about 25 minutes (roughly two months after the animal flight).
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgolfier_brothers ; https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/artificial-clouds-and-inflammable-air-the-science-and-spectacle-of-the-first-balloon-flights-1783/

## Notes / conflicts
- Flight duration of the animal flight: most sources say about 8 minutes; one popular article says 15. Script uses eight (Science History Institute + Wikipedia).
- Injury story (sheep kicked the rooster vs the duck) differs between Guinness and BBML (https://www.bbml.org.uk/the-day-a-duck-a-sheep-and-a-cockerel-took-to-the-air-in-1783/); not used.
- Science History Institute says Gibraltar was "then held by Spain"; it was British (Great Siege). Not used in this script.
- The "shirt over the fire" and "September vs November 1782" versions of the first experiment vary; script avoids an exact month for it.
- No invented quotes: the script has no character dialogue.
